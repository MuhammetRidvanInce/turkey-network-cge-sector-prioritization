\subsection{Theoretical Gap, Original Contribution, and Methodological Rigor}
\label{sec:theoretical-gap}

The existing literature either assumes that topological position carries macroeconomic importance or takes topology as given when producing general-equilibrium outcomes. Different theoretical mechanisms underlying this approach have been developed by \citep{ref-hulten1978,ref-gabaix2011,ref-acemoglu2012,ref-carvalho2014,ref-baqaee-farhi2019a}. The empirical relationship between these two analytical approaches, however, has not been systematically tested using the same data, sectoral universe, and measurement discipline. In particular, whether standard production-network centrality measures predict size-controlled general-equilibrium impacts out of sample within the same set of sectors remains an open question. Although studies such as \citep{ref-baqaee-farhi2020a,ref-fadinger2022} provide a theoretical foundation, the empirical strength of this relationship in a medium-scale applied model has not been established.
